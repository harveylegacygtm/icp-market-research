# Pain & Desire Depth Ladder

Not the ICP's 4 layers (Firmographics, Technographics, Buying Signals,
Psychographics — that's who they are). This is a different stack: how deep one
pain actually runs, for the person feeling it. Run it on the top 2-3 primary
pains from Step 3's diagnostic only. Do not ladder every pain found — it
produces a bloated, repetitive doc for no extra insight. Pick the 2-3 that are
most severe or most frequently mentioned in the research.

## The method: laddering

For each of the top 2-3 pains, keep asking one question: "if they solved this,
what does that actually get them — or if they don't, what does it cost them
beyond the obvious?" Climb the ladder one rung at a time. Stop when the answer
stops changing (you've hit bedrock) or you're inventing psychology with no
evidence — mark those layers "inferred," don't skip them, but don't sell them
as verified either.

## The layers

1. **Logical / practical**: the surface benefit. Save money, save time, make
   more money, reduce risk. This is usually what the prospect says out loud
   first, and what most cold email already targets.
2. **Emotional**: what changes about how they feel day to day. Less stress,
   more confidence, relief, consistency, a sense of control instead of
   whack-a-mole.
3. **Status / social**: what it means in front of other people. Beating a
   competitor, looking sharp to a boss or board, industry reputation, being
   the person who fixed it, a promotion or bigger scope.
4. **Personal / identity**: what it frees up in their actual life. Time back
   for family, evenings, not dreading Mondays, proof to themselves they're
   good at the job, avoiding burnout.
5. **Legacy (optional, don't force it)**: only include if the research
   actually supports it — what this means for the kind of leader or company
   they become, or their longer arc. Most pains never need this layer. If you
   have to reach for it, leave it out.

## Evidence standard

Layer 1 should almost always trace to a direct quote or data point from the
research (forum post, review, case study). Layers 2-4 are often inferred from
context — tone of the quote, what the person emphasizes, what they complain
about besides the practical cost. Say so: "inferred from the frustration in
the tone of [source]," not stated as fact. This matches the skill's existing
confidence-flagging standard (high/medium/low, verified vs. inferred).

## Output format

For each of the 2-3 pains laddered:

**Pain: [name it in the prospect's own words]**
- Logical: ... (source: [quote/URL] or "inferred")
- Emotional: ... (source or "inferred")
- Status: ... (source or "inferred")
- Personal: ... (source or "inferred")
- Legacy (if applicable): ...

The bottom layers are where the sharpest hooks and subject lines come from —
"save 10 hours a week" is Layer 1 and everyone says it; "stop being the person
who has to explain the missed deadline again" is Layer 3-4 and almost nobody
says it.
